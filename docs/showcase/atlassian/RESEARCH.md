# Atlassian (NASDAQ: TEAM) — Public research brief for BlockID.au showcase

> **Purpose.** Case study #1 for the BlockID.au demo library. Every fact
> below is cited from public sources so the platform can walk a founder
> through Atlassian's 12-phase journey with verifiable numbers. Compiled
> 2026-07-21 from three parallel research subagents. Source URLs are
> preserved throughout.

---

## Why Atlassian first

- **Australian** — Cannon-Brookes + Farquhar founded in Sydney 2002.
- **Publicly listed** — NASDAQ:TEAM since 2015-12-10 → deep SEC EDGAR
  paper trail (F-1, 10-K, DEF 14A, 8-K).
- **Bootstrap → unicorn → decacorn** — spans every phase of BlockID.au's
  12-phase startup journey with clear inflection points.
- **Governance singularity** — dual-class + co-CEO + evergreen ESOP
  survived from IPO through decacorn (still ~42.7% founder voting).
- **Aussie-specific** — UK-PLC intermediate step (2014) then Delaware
  redomiciliation (2022) is the canonical path an Australian founder
  contemplating a US listing will need to model.

## 4. ESOP mechanics

- **Plan**: 2015 Share Incentive Plan (Class A) + 2015 ESPP.
- **Initial reserve at IPO**: 20,700,000 Class A for incentive plan +
  5,700,000 Class A for ESPP.
- **Outstanding at IPO**: 15,288,310 Class A options (WA exercise
  $2.23), 9,758,363 Class A RSUs, 1,552,500 Class B options ($0.51).
- **Evergreen** annual reserve refresh built into both plans.
- **Vesting**: multi-year, 4-year standard; retention grants attached
  to acquisitions (Trello ~$65M, OpsGenie $36.3M, Halp $4.1M, Loom
  ~$95M) all continue-vesting.
- **ESPP**: 6-month offering periods, 15% discount, based on lower of
  offering-date/purchase-date price (per 20-F filings).

Sources: F-1/A + FY24 Annual Report —
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000165037224000049/team2024annualreport.pdf

## 5. Dual-class structure

- **Class A** 1 vote/share; publicly floated.
- **Class B** 10 votes/share; held by founders + insiders + Accel.
- Class B controlled **~96.7% of voting power immediately post-IPO**
  despite being a minority of economic interest.
- Conversion 1:1 into Class A; automatic on transfer to a non-permitted
  holder; voluntary at any time.
- **No time-based or ownership-based sunset** provision in original
  prospectus.
- Founders sell under Rule **10b5-1 plans**; Cannon-Brookes disclosed
  47,066,808 Class B shares in 2026-03-31 13G/A.

Source: 424B4 prospectus —
https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1650372/000104746915009143/a2226831z424b4.htm

## 8. Culture + hackathon + Pledge 1%

- **5 core values** (2007): "Open company, no bullshit"; "Build with
  heart and balance"; "Don't #@!% the customer"; "Play as a team"; "Be
  the change you seek."
- **ShipIt hackathon** — 24h cross-functional quarterly since 2005.
- **Team Playbook** — public library of "plays" + Health Monitor.
- **Pledge 1%** — 1% profit/product/equity/employee-time to Foundation.

## 11. Key insights for BlockID.au

**A. Bootstrap-then-secondary preserves founder equity.** From 2002 to
2010 Atlassian took zero outside money. When it finally accepted Accel's
US$60M in 2010, it was 100% secondary — no dilution to remaining
holders — used purely for founder + employee liquidity and to seed board
governance. Four years later, T. Rowe Price US$150M was again pure
secondary. Founders each still held ~37% at IPO — roughly 3–5× what a
comparable US SaaS founder retains through a normal Series A/B/C stack.

**B. Dual-class + evergreen ESOP = keep control at IPO.** Class B
retained 96.7% of voting power at IPO despite being a minority of the
economic interest. A decade later Cannon-Brookes alone still controls
~42.7% of aggregate voting power. Meanwhile the 2015 Share Incentive
Plan and ESPP both have evergreen provisions so ESOP is continuously
topped up. **BlockID.au's Phase 4 (Equity design) should model dual-class
+ evergreen ESOP as the founder-preserving default for Aussie founders**,
mirroring Atlassian's move to NASDAQ specifically because ASX prohibits
dual-class at IPO.

**C. Acquisition template — 90% cash + 10% retention equity.** Every
disclosed Atlassian M&A is ~90% cash + ~10% restricted shares/RSUs for
target-company key employees on continuing vest. No megastock deals.
Combined with the buyback programs ($1.0B 2023, $1.5B 2024) and zero
dividend policy the equity story is: reinvest into growth, retire
dilution mechanically, keep voting concentrated with founders.

**D. Product-led growth beats sales-led for a developer buyer.**
Atlassian hit US$1M revenue in year 1, US$50M ARR in year 8, US$100M
revenue by ~2011 **without a traditional sales team**. American Airlines
bought Jira via the website in 2003 with zero human contact. BlockID.au
parallel: founders and lawyers should self-serve equity/cap-table/token
flows exactly the same way.

**E. Governance sequencing matters more than governance perfection at
IPO.** Atlassian went public with a 5-member board, 3 independent, no
sunset provision on Class B. It progressively hardened governance
post-IPO: independent Chair, three standing committees, ex-LinkedIn CFO
chairing Audit. BlockID.au's IPO-track score should therefore weight:
(a) dual-class feasibility, (b) NED bench readiness, (c) committee
readiness, (d) whether founders hold enough Class B-equivalent equity to
survive multiple rounds.
