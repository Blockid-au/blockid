# Startup Investor Readiness Checklist Australia (2026)

*By the BlockID Research Team — updated July 2026*

**TL;DR**

- Investor readiness is the difference between a two-week close and a six-month grind. Most AU seed rounds die not from a bad idea but from a missing data room and a shaky cap table.
- This checklist covers **pre-seed, seed, and Series A** — with different bars at each stage. Do not present pre-seed evidence to a Series A investor and expect a term sheet.
- At minimum you need: **clean cap table, ASIC-current constitution, signed founder agreements, IP assigned, 3-year model with unit economics, data room, and a 2-minute investor summary**.
- ESIC registration + R&D Tax Incentive engagement typically shorten the AU raise cycle by 4–6 weeks because they de-risk investor due diligence.

## Why "investor ready" matters more in AU

Australian venture capital is smaller and more cautious than the US market. Cut Through Venture's 2026 monthly reports show that AU deal counts are ~15% of the US equivalent per capita, and that AU investors do **more diligence per dollar** than their US counterparts. That extra scrutiny means the shortcuts that work in San Francisco — "we'll clean up governance after we close" — will kill your round in Sydney.

Being investor ready = being able to answer any reasonable investor question **from your data room in under 60 seconds**, without a scramble. If your CFO or founder needs three days to produce your MRR-by-cohort table, you're not ready.

## Section 1 — Legal & governance

Every AU investor asks for these first. Missing any single item stalls the deal.

- [ ] **Pty Ltd registered with ASIC** — company name, ACN, ABN, registered office current.
- [ ] **Constitution reviewed in last 12 months** — most off-the-shelf constitutions don't accommodate SAFE conversions, ESOP plans, or drag-along rights. Fix these *before* raising, not during.
- [ ] **Founder Vesting Agreements** — 4-year vesting with 1-year cliff is standard for AU seed. Investors will insist on this if it's missing.
- [ ] **IP Assignment Deeds** — every founder, contractor, and employee has signed an IP-assignment deed transferring all work-product to the company. Missing IP assignments are the #1 reason AU rounds die at diligence.
- [ ] **Employment / contractor agreements current** — with confidentiality, IP, and restraint clauses appropriate to role.
- [ ] **ESOP scheme documented** — plan rules approved by the board, option grants recorded, ATO ESS reporting current.
- [ ] **Directors' resolutions filed** — for all share issues, option grants, and material contracts.
- [ ] **ASIC lodgements up to date** — annual review paid, share allotments (Form 484) lodged within 28 days.
- [ ] **Register of Members and Register of Option Holders** — maintained and reconciled to your cap table.

## Section 2 — Cap table & equity

The cap table is the first artefact a VC opens. It reveals more about your judgement than your pitch deck.

- [ ] **Single source of truth** — one spreadsheet or tool (not five). We recommend the [workspace cap table](/workspace/cap-table) so founders, lawyers, and investors work from the same numbers.
- [ ] **Fully diluted view** — including all issued options, unallocated option pool, SAFE conversions, and warrants.
- [ ] **Waterfall model** — what each shareholder receives at A$5M, A$20M, A$100M, A$500M exit scenarios.
- [ ] **ESOP pool ≥10%** — investors typically require 10–15% unallocated pool *pre-money* at seed.
- [ ] **No dead equity** — early advisors or ex-cofounders sitting on >5% with no ongoing contribution is a red flag. Buy them out or negotiate down before raising.
- [ ] **Founder ownership ≥60% pre-round** at seed — VCs get nervous below this because it caps future dilution capacity.

## Section 3 — Financial model & unit economics

Investors don't fund revenue; they fund the **path to it**. Your model has to be a scenario tool, not a hockey-stick.

- [ ] **3–5 year P&L, cash flow, and balance sheet** — driver-based, not just percentage growth on last year.
- [ ] **Monthly for the current year, quarterly thereafter**.
- [ ] **Assumptions tab** — every driver (CAC, ACV, churn, ARPA, headcount, salary bands) listed and sourced.
- [ ] **Base / bear / bull scenarios** — with what triggers each.
- [ ] **Unit economics**: CAC, LTV, LTV:CAC (target ≥3:1), payback period (target <18 months), gross margin.
- [ ] **Cohort retention table** by month or quarter.
- [ ] **Burn multiple** — net burn ÷ net new ARR (target <2× at seed, <1.5× at Series A).
- [ ] **Runway** — months of cash at current burn, and at post-raise burn.
- [ ] **GST / BAS reconciliation** — model must reconcile to lodged BAS returns.

An AU investor will spend 20 minutes in your model before your first meeting. If it doesn't foot to your bank statements, you won't get a second one.

## Section 4 — Traction & metrics

Different stages, different evidence:

| Stage | Minimum traction bar |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-seed | Prototype + 5–10 problem-validation interviews; letter of intent from 1–2 design partners |
| Seed | A$10k–A$50k MRR (SaaS) or 5,000+ MAU; measurable retention; repeatable acquisition channel |
| Series A | A$1M+ ARR, growing 15%+ MoM or 3× YoY; net revenue retention >100% for SaaS; clear playbook |

- [ ] **KPI dashboard** — accessible to investors, refreshed weekly minimum.
- [ ] **Cohort analysis** — retention, ARPA expansion, contraction, churn.
- [ ] **Pipeline / funnel data** — conversion rates at each stage.
- [ ] **Customer testimonials + logos** — permission secured in writing.
- [ ] **NPS or CSAT score** — with methodology.

## Section 6 — Compliance & tax posture

- [ ] **ESIC registration** — if eligible, register with ATO. Read the [ESIC and R&D Tax Incentive guide](/insights/esic-and-rnd-tax-incentive-guide-2026) for the eligibility gate.
- [ ] **R&D Tax Incentive** — register annually with AusIndustry (30 April deadline for June year-end companies).
- [ ] **GST registration** — mandatory once turnover exceeds A$75k.
- [ ] **PAYG withholding + Single Touch Payroll** — current.
- [ ] **Superannuation** — 11.5% (FY26) paid quarterly; no missed quarters.
- [ ] **Privacy Policy + APP compliance** — mandatory if turnover >A$3M or handling health/credit data.
- [ ] **AFSL disclaimer** — if any content touches financial products.

## Section 7 — Investor materials

- [ ] **10–12 slide pitch deck** — problem, solution, market, traction, business model, GTM, team, ask, use of funds.
- [ ] **2-minute Loom / video walkthrough** of product.
- [ ] **One-page investor summary** — for warm intros.
- [ ] **Ask + use of funds table** — line-item budget for the round.
- [ ] **12-month milestone plan** — what the money buys.
- [ ] **Target investor list** — 30–60 investors, prioritised by fit, with warm-intro path documented.

## Section 8 — The 60-second test

For each question below, can you produce the answer *from your data room* in under 60 seconds?

1. What is your current MRR and last-month growth rate?
2. What is your CAC, LTV, and payback period, by channel?
3. Who are your top 5 customers and what is their ARR contribution?
4. What is your fully diluted cap table today, and post-round?
5. What is your monthly burn and runway?
6. Who are your top 3 competitors and how are you differentiated?
7. What is your ESIC status?
8. Where is your IP legally owned?
9. Who has signed vesting agreements and on what schedule?
10. What is your churn rate (logo and revenue), and why do customers leave?

Any "let me get back to you" is a red flag to the investor. Practise until every answer is instant.

## Common AU-specific pitfalls

- **Missing IP assignments** from contractors who built early prototypes.
- **Founder shares issued without vesting** — hard to retrofit without founder consent.
- **Constitution missing drag-along** — a single small shareholder can block a future exit.
- **ESOP plan documented but no ATO ESS reports lodged** — triggers tax problems for employees.
- **R&D Tax claim inflated** — AusIndustry audits are increasing; be defensible.
- **Cap table doesn't reconcile to ASIC** — instant credibility loss.

## FAQ

### How long does an AU seed raise typically take?
3–6 months from first meeting to funds in bank, assuming you're investor ready. Add 2–3 months if you're not.

### Do I need a lawyer before I start raising?
Yes. AU startup lawyers charge A$3k–A$10k for a pre-raise readiness pack (constitution, shareholders' agreement template, ESOP plan). It's the highest-ROI legal spend you'll ever make.

### Should I raise a SAFE or a priced round?
SAFEs suit pre-seed rounds under A$1M and single-investor angel rounds. Priced rounds are standard from seed upward. AU-adapted SAFE templates from AVCAL are the safest starting point.

### What's the biggest reason AU seed rounds fail at diligence?
Broken IP chain — a contractor or ex-cofounder retained rights that were never assigned. Fix this before you send a deck.

### How much runway should I raise?
18–24 months at seed, 24–36 months at Series A. Anything less and you're back on the road within 12 months.

### Do I need audited financials at seed?
No. Management accounts + BAS reconciliation are enough. Audits become expected at Series A.

### Should I disclose competing offers?
Yes — professionally. "We have another term sheet at similar terms; we'd prefer to work with you if you can commit by Friday" is a legitimate close.
