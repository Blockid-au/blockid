The higher the capital requirement relative to your valuation, the more equity you surrender.

## The Math of the Cap Table

Your ownership isn't fixed; it's a fraction of the total shares outstanding. Pre-money valuation is what the company is worth *before* the check; post-money is that value *plus* the cash invested.

| Scenario | Pre-Money Val | Investment | Post-Money Val | Founder Equity |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Conservative** | A$2.0M | A$500K | A$2.5M | 80% |
| **Aggressive** | A$5.0M | A$1.0M | A$6.0M | 83.3% |
| **Down Round** | A$4.0M | A$1.0M | A$5.0M | 60% (est.) |
| **Over-funded** | A$1.0M | A$1.0M | A$2.0M | 50% |

## How to Prevent Equity Erosion

Avoid "valuation vanity." Raising too much at too high a price creates a valuation ceiling that triggers devastating down-rounds later.

* **Set a "Dilution Ceiling"** — Cap your total equity giveaway per round (typically 15-25%).
* **Prioritize Revenue Growth** — High growth justifies higher valuations without needing more cash.
* **Use Convertible Notes** — Delay valuation discussions until you have more leverage.
* **Model "What-If" Scenarios** — Simulate a 20% drop in valuation before signing a term sheet.
* **Optimize Burn Rate** — Lower spend means less frequent fundraising and less dilution.
