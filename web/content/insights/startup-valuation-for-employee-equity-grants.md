Incorrect valuations trigger immediate ATO tax liabilities for employees before they've seen a cent of profit.

## The "Fair Market Value" Trap

The ATO requires "Fair Market Value" (FMV) for share issues. If you set the strike price too low without justification, the difference is taxed as income immediately.

| Stage | Valuation Method | Typical Price Driver | ATO Risk Level |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Pre-Seed** | Cost-to-Duplicate | Asset/Team Value | Low |
| **Seed** | Scorecard / Berkus | Traction/Milestones | Medium |
| **Series A** | Revenue Multiples | ARR / Growth Rate | High |
| **Late Stage** | DCF / Comparable | EBITDA / Market Cap | High |

## How to set the strike price

To avoid tax surprises and excessive dilution, follow this execution checklist:

* **Audit your cap table** to determine total shares outstanding.
* **Benchmark your SVI Score** to justify the valuation to the ATO.
* **Set a strike price** equal to the current FMV to avoid "upfront" tax.
* **Utilize the Startup Concession** (ESS) to defer tax until a liquidity event.
* **Cap the option pool** at 10-15% to prevent founder dilution.
* **Document the valuation** logic in a formal board minute.
