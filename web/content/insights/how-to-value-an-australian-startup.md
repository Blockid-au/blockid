# How to Value an Australian Startup: A 2026 Founder's Guide

*By the BlockID Research Team — updated July 2026*

**TL;DR**

- Australian pre-seed valuations in 2026 typically sit in the **A$1M–A$4M** pre-money range; seed rounds cluster at **A$4M–A$12M**; Series A rounds land between **A$15M–A$40M** post-money for software startups (Cut Through Venture / Folklore Ventures quarterly reports).
- No single method is "correct". Sophisticated investors triangulate **DCF, Berkus, Scorecard, VC method, and comparable rounds** — you should present two or three that support the same range.
- For pre-revenue AU startups, **Scorecard + Berkus** are the most defensible; once you have >A$50k MRR, revenue multiples and DCF start to matter more than qualitative frameworks.
- Registering as an **ESIC** unlocks a 20% investor tax offset (up to A$200k) — this materially widens your addressable investor pool and often lifts the valuation ceiling.

## Why valuation is a negotiation, not a calculation

Valuation is what a willing investor pays a willing founder — full stop. Every framework below produces a *defensible number to negotiate around*, not a market clearing price. Australian founders often over-index on DCF spreadsheets and under-invest in the two things that actually move a term sheet: **traction evidence** and **the alternative** (i.e., a competing offer).

Before you pick a method, be clear on the question you're answering:

| You are… | Use these methods first |
| --- | --- |
| Pre-revenue, pre-product | Berkus, Scorecard, Risk-Factor Summation |
| Post-launch, <A$50k MRR | Scorecard, VC Method, comparable AU rounds |
| Post-A$50k MRR, growing >10% MoM | Revenue multiples, VC Method, DCF sanity-check |
| Profitable / late-stage | DCF, EBITDA multiples, precedent M&A |

## Method 1 — Discounted Cash Flow (DCF)

DCF forecasts free cash flow for 5–10 years, discounts it back to present value using a risk-adjusted rate, then adds a terminal value. It is the *only* method grounded in first principles — every other method is a shortcut.

**The catch for startups:** small errors in growth or churn assumptions compound violently. A 5-year DCF built on "we'll hit A$10M ARR by year 3" is a fiction unless you can defend each cohort. Use DCF as a **sanity check** on the range produced by simpler methods, not as your headline number.

Typical discount rates AU VCs apply to early-stage companies:

| Stage | Discount rate | Rationale |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pre-seed | 50–70% | High mortality; team + idea only |
| Seed | 40–60% | Product exists; PMF unproven |
| Series A | 30–50% | Repeatable acquisition; unit economics visible |
| Series B+ | 20–35% | Scaling known playbook |

Discount rates like these compress a "big year-5 number" into something modest today. If your DCF still shows a large valuation after applying a 50% discount, either your assumptions are aggressive or you have a genuinely large opportunity — either way, be prepared to defend every input line by line.

## Method 2 — The Berkus Method (pre-revenue)

Dave Berkus's framework caps a pre-revenue startup at roughly A$3M by assigning up to ~A$600k of value to each of five buckets:

| Value driver | Max value (A$) |
| --- | --- |
| Sound idea (base value) | 600,000 |
| Working prototype (reduces tech risk) | 600,000 |
| Quality management team | 600,000 |
| Strategic relationships (reduces market risk) | 600,000 |
| Product rollout or sales (reduces production risk) | 600,000 |

Berkus is deliberately conservative — it stops you talking yourself into a A$10M pre-money on a slide deck. The right way to use it is to score each bucket honestly, defend the score with evidence, and treat the total as a **floor**.

## Method 3 — Scorecard Method (Bill Payne)

The Scorecard method takes the median pre-money valuation of recent AU pre-seed rounds — currently around **A$2.5M** — and multiplies it by a weighted score across seven factors, based on how your startup compares to the peer average (1.0 = average, >1.0 = above, <1.0 = below).

| Factor | Typical weight |
| --- | --- |
| Strength of management team | 30% |
| Size of opportunity | 25% |
| Product / technology | 15% |
| Competitive environment | 10% |
| Marketing / sales / partnerships | 10% |
| Need for additional investment | 5% |
| Other (e.g., customer feedback, ESIC status) | 5% |

**Worked example.** A Sydney SaaS founder with a strong domain team (1.25×), large TAM (1.10×), average product (1.00×), thin partnerships (0.80×) produces a weighted multiplier of ~1.06. Applied to a A$2.5M regional median, the Scorecard valuation lands near **A$2.65M pre-money** — a number both sides can walk into a first meeting with.

## Method 4 — VC Method

Popularised by Bill Sahlman, the VC method works backwards from a plausible exit:

1. Estimate exit value in year 5–7 (e.g., 5× revenue on A$20M ARR = A$100M).
2. Apply the VC's target return (typically 10× at seed, 5× at Series A).
3. Divide to get today's *post-money* valuation.
4. Subtract the round size to get *pre-money*.

Example: A$100M exit / 10× return = A$10M post-money. If the founder is raising A$1.5M, pre-money = **A$8.5M**. Adjust downward for the expected dilution stack across future rounds (typically 40–60% additional dilution before exit).

This method is what almost every AU seed VC (Blackbird, Square Peg, Airtree, Folklore, King River) actually uses internally, even if their term sheets cite Scorecard. If you can walk a VC through your VC-method calculation with realistic exit comparables, you shift the conversation from "how much are you worth?" to "what return does this deliver?".

## Method 5 — Comparable AU rounds

The most persuasive input in any AU raise is **what similar startups raised in the last 12 months**. Sources founders should cite:

- **Cut Through Venture** monthly deal reports (state of AU/NZ private capital).
- **Folklore Ventures** state-of-play report (semi-annual).
- **Crunchbase / Dealroom** filtered to AU HQ.
- **ASIC extracts** — new share issues are public and readable.

Examples worth studying (all publicly announced):

| Startup | Sector | Stage | Round | Reported valuation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Canva (early) | Design SaaS | Series A (2013) | ~US$3M | ~US$18M post-money |
| Airwallex | Fintech | Series A (2017) | US$13M | Undisclosed (small) |
| Zeller | Fintech | Series A (2021) | A$50M | ~A$400M post-money |
| Employment Hero | HR SaaS | Series F (2022) | A$263M | A$1.25B post-money |
| Immutable | Web3 gaming | Series C (2022) | US$200M | US$2.5B post-money |

The pattern is clear: **valuation follows verified traction**. Employment Hero didn't hit unicorn on the pitch — it hit it on the ARR curve.

## Putting the numbers together

A defensible AU seed valuation deck usually shows three methods converging on a range. For example:

- Scorecard: A$5.2M
- VC method: A$6.1M
- Comparable rounds (median AU seed 2026): A$5.8M
- **Ask: A$5–7M pre-money**, targeting A$1.5M raise for 20–25% dilution.

Overshoot the range and you'll struggle to close; undershoot and you give away equity you'll never claw back at Series A. ## Australia-specific levers that move valuation

Foreign frameworks miss three things that materially change AU founder economics:

1. **ESIC status.** Early Stage Innovation Company registration gives investors a 20% non-refundable tax offset (up to A$200k) and CGT exemption on shares held >12 months. This widens your investor pool and typically supports a 10–20% valuation premium. See the ESIC gate above the market.
2. **R&D Tax Incentive.** For entities with <A$20M turnover, the refundable offset is 43.5% of eligible R&D spend. That's real cash — factor it into runway before you set a raise amount.
3. **AFSL / accredited investor rules.** Australia's "sophisticated investor" thresholds (A$2.5M net assets or A$250k income) determine who can legally see your deck without disclosure — and most seed capital comes from sophisticateds. Structure your raise accordingly.

## Common valuation mistakes AU founders make

- **Anchoring to US comparables.** SF seed valuations run 2–3× AU medians. Use AU data.
- **Confusing pre-money and post-money.** Post-money = pre-money + round. A 25% offer on a A$4M pre-money = A$1M raise; that same 25% on a A$4M *post-money* = only A$750k.
- **Ignoring the option pool shuffle.** Investors usually require a 10–15% unallocated ESOP *before* investment — that comes out of your dilution, not theirs.
- **Overweighting DCF.** Nobody buys a pre-revenue DCF. Lead with Scorecard + comparables.
- **Refusing to walk away.** The best valuation lever is a credible alternative offer.

## FAQ

### What is a "fair" valuation for an AU pre-seed startup in 2026?
Median AU pre-seed pre-money is around A$2M–A$4M (Cut Through Venture Q1 2026 data). Strong technical teams with a working prototype and ESIC status can push to A$5M+.

### Do I need a formal valuation report to raise?
No. Investors use the frameworks above internally. A formal Independent Expert Report is only required for ASX-related transactions, related-party deals, or where the constitution mandates one.

### How does ESIC status affect valuation?
It doesn't directly increase valuation, but it widens the pool of angels willing to invest (thanks to the 20% tax offset), which typically firms up the price at the top of your range.

### Should I share my valuation model with investors?
Yes — but share the *range* and the methods, not just a headline number. Transparency builds trust; a spreadsheet with three methods converging on the same range is disarming.

### How much equity should I give away at seed?
15–25% is standard. Over 30% at seed leaves you dangerously diluted by Series B. Our [SVI report](/svi) models forward dilution across the phases.

### Can I use SAFEs or convertibles to defer valuation?
Yes — SAFEs with a valuation cap defer the priced-round conversation, which is useful when you can't defend a strong number yet. Cap them realistically or you'll re-negotiate at conversion anyway.

### What multiples do AU SaaS startups trade on?
Public AU SaaS trades on 6–10× forward revenue in 2026 (down from the 15–25× peak of 2021). Private early-stage rounds usually price at 8–15× *forward* ARR when growth exceeds 100% YoY.
