This is one of the most common raise-blocking due-diligence failures at Series A. Have this reviewed by an Australian-qualified lawyer before execution. ## Recitals

A. The Contractor has been engaged by the Company under a services agreement dated {{services_agreement_date}} (the **Services Agreement**) to perform certain work for the Company (the **Services**), as more particularly described in {{services_description}}.

B. In the course of performing the Services, the Contractor has created, or may in future create, works, code, designs, materials, and other output (the **Deliverables**) in which Intellectual Property Rights subsist.

C. The Parties intend that the Company own all Intellectual Property Rights in the Deliverables. Because the Contractor is an independent contractor (and not an employee), those rights do not vest in the Company by default under section 35(6) of the Copyright Act 1968 (Cth) or otherwise, and this Deed is executed to effect the assignment.

D. This Deed operates as a stand-alone assignment and does not depend on the continued operation of the Services Agreement.

## 1. Assignment of Intellectual Property

### 1.1 Present assignment
The Contractor, with effect from the Effective Date, assigns to the Company absolutely all right, title, and interest (including all Intellectual Property Rights, whether registered or unregistered, existing anywhere in the world, present and future) in and to the Deliverables and any and all Foreground IP (defined in section 2), including without limitation:

1. all copyright subsisting in software, source code, documentation, technical drawings, designs, artwork, marketing collateral, audio-visual works, and databases, in each case under the **Copyright Act 1968** (Cth);
2. all rights in any invention, discovery, or improvement (whether patentable or not) under the **Patents Act 1990** (Cth);
3. all rights in registered and unregistered designs under the **Designs Act 2003** (Cth);
4. all rights in trade marks, brand identifiers, and get-up under the **Trade Marks Act 1995** (Cth) and at common law;
5. all rights in circuit layouts under the **Circuit Layouts Act 1989** (Cth);
6. all rights in confidential information, trade secrets, know-how, methodologies, processes, algorithms, and data compilations; and
7. all rights of a similar nature to the foregoing, anywhere in the world.

### 1.2 Retrospective effect
To the fullest extent permissible at law, the assignment in section 1.1 has effect from the date on which each item of Foreground IP was first created. The Contractor holds all such rights on trust for the Company from the date of creation to the Effective Date and any beneficial interest vests in the Company on the Effective Date.

### 1.3 Future IP
Any Foreground IP created by the Contractor after the Effective Date in the course of, or in connection with, the Services vests in the Company automatically on creation, without further act. If any such assignment is not effective at law, the Contractor holds those rights on trust for the Company pending assignment.

### 1.4 Background IP
The Contractor retains ownership of all Intellectual Property Rights created or acquired by the Contractor **before** the Effective Date, or **outside** the scope of the Services (the **Background IP**). To the extent that Background IP is incorporated in, or is necessary to use, any Deliverable, the Contractor grants the Company a **perpetual, worldwide, irrevocable, royalty-free, sublicensable, non-exclusive licence** to use, copy, modify, and exploit that Background IP as part of the Company's use of the Deliverables.

### 1.5 Third-party / open-source components
The Contractor must not incorporate into any Deliverable any material owned by a third party (including any Creative Commons, GPL, LGPL, AGPL, or other copyleft-licensed material) that would encumber the Company's ability to distribute the Deliverables under the Company's chosen commercial terms, unless the Contractor has disclosed that material in Annexure A and the Company has consented in writing.

## 2. Definitions

**Deliverables** has the meaning given in recital B.

**Foreground IP** means all Intellectual Property Rights created by or on behalf of the Contractor in the course of, or in connection with, the Services, including in the Deliverables.

**Background IP** has the meaning given in section 1.4.

**Intellectual Property Rights** means all industrial and intellectual property rights of any kind, whether registered or unregistered, arising anywhere in the world, including without limitation the rights described in section 1.1.

## 3. Moral rights consent

### 3.1 Consent
Where the Contractor is a natural person, the Contractor is the **author** (within the meaning of Part IX of the **Copyright Act 1968** (Cth)) of the copyright works comprising the Foreground IP. The Contractor consents, unconditionally and irrevocably, under section 195AW and section 195AWA of the Copyright Act to all acts or omissions by the Company (and its licensees, successors, and assigns) that would, but for this consent, infringe any of the Contractor's moral rights (as defined in section 189 of the Copyright Act) in any existing or future works.

### 3.2 Contractor-as-entity
Where the Contractor is an entity (not a natural person), the Contractor warrants that:
1. it has obtained written moral-rights consents from each natural-person author who contributed to the Foreground IP in favour of the Company on materially the same terms as section 3.1; and
2. it will procure equivalent consents from any future contributor.

### 3.3 Scope
The consent given (or procured) under this section 3 extends to:
1. the right of **attribution** of authorship (section 193);
2. the right **not to have authorship falsely attributed** (section 195AC); and
3. the right of **integrity of authorship** (section 195AI),

in respect of any and all uses, modifications, adaptations, translations, derivative works, or dispositions of the Foreground IP.

## 4. Warranties

### 4.1 Contractor warranties
The Contractor warrants to the Company, as at the Effective Date and continuing, that:

1. the Contractor is the sole legal and beneficial owner of the Foreground IP and has full authority to assign it;
2. no part of the Foreground IP infringes the rights of any third party, and no third party has any claim, lien, encumbrance, licence, or right of pre-emption over the Foreground IP;
3. the Contractor has not previously assigned, licensed, or purported to assign or license any of the Foreground IP to any other person;
4. any Background IP incorporated in the Deliverables is fully owned by the Contractor and can be licensed to the Company as contemplated in section 1.4; and
5. all persons (including subcontractors, employees, and consultants of the Contractor) who have contributed or will contribute to the Foreground IP have assigned (or will assign) their rights to the Contractor and have consented (or will consent) to their moral rights being subject to section 3.

### 4.2 Sham-contracting representation
The Contractor represents that the engagement between the Parties is genuinely one of principal and independent contractor, and not employment, having regard to the multi-indicia test applied by Australian courts (as reaffirmed in *CFMMEU v Personnel Contracting* [2022] HCA 1 and *ZG Operations v Jamsek* [2022] HCA 2). The Parties acknowledge that misclassification exposes the Company to sham-contracting penalties under Part 3-1 of the **Fair Work Act 2009** (Cth).

### 4.3 Survival
The warranties in this section 4 survive execution of this Deed and any termination or expiry of the Services Agreement.

## 5. Further assurances

### 5.1 Registration and perfection
The Contractor must, at the Company's cost, promptly do all things and execute all documents (including any assignment, deed of confirmation, patent-application transfer, or trade-mark assignment form) that the Company reasonably requests to:
1. vest the Foreground IP in the Company;
2. register the Company as the owner of any registered or applied-for Intellectual Property Rights within the Foreground IP; or
3. record the assignment on any register maintained by IP Australia or any equivalent overseas office.

### 5.2 Power of attorney
The Contractor irrevocably appoints the Company (and each of its directors from time to time) as the Contractor's attorney to execute in the Contractor's name any document the Company considers necessary to give effect to section 5.1. The Contractor ratifies and confirms anything the attorney does under this power. This power is granted for valuable consideration (namely the fees payable under the Services Agreement) and is granted by way of security.

### 5.3 PPSR
Where any part of the Foreground IP constitutes personal property under the **Personal Property Securities Act 2009** (Cth), the Contractor consents to the Company registering the assignment on the Personal Property Securities Register.

## 6. Confidentiality

The Contractor must keep confidential all confidential information forming part of the Foreground IP, the Deliverables, and the Company's business. The Contractor must not use such information other than for the benefit of the Company. This obligation survives the termination or expiry of the Services Agreement indefinitely.

## 7. Consideration and payment

The Parties acknowledge that the consideration for the assignment made under this Deed is included in the fees payable to the Contractor under the Services Agreement. No further payment is required for the assignment. This section is included for the avoidance of doubt only — this document is executed as a **deed** and does not require consideration to be enforceable.
