This Constitution is adopted under section 136 of the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth) (the **Corporations Act**). Upon adoption, and to the extent permitted by law, this Constitution **displaces the replaceable rules** in the Corporations Act (per s135(2)) other than those which apply to the Company as a mandatory rule. This Constitution has effect as a contract between the Company and each member, between the Company and each director and company secretary, and between members among themselves (per s140).

Words defined in the Corporations Act have the same meaning in this Constitution unless the context requires otherwise.

## 1. Preliminary

### 1.1 Name and type
The name of the Company is **{{company_name}} Pty Ltd**. It is a proprietary company limited by shares.

### 1.2 Replaceable rules displaced
The replaceable rules referred to in s135 of the Corporations Act do not apply to the Company. Where a provision of this Constitution is inconsistent with the replaceable rules, this Constitution prevails to the extent of that inconsistency.

### 1.3 Definitions and interpretation
In this Constitution:
- **Board** means the directors acting collectively as the board of the Company;
- **Business Day** means a day (other than a Saturday, Sunday, or public holiday) on which trading banks are open for business in Sydney, New South Wales;
- **Change of Control** means a transaction or series of related transactions resulting in a person (together with their Associates as defined in s11 of the Corporations Act) acquiring more than 50% of the Voting Shares of the Company or all or substantially all of its assets;
- **Chair** has the meaning given in clause 8.6;
- **Company** means {{company_name}} Pty Ltd;
- **Constitution** means this constitution as amended from time to time;
- **Corporations Act** means the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth);
- **Directors** means the directors for the time being of the Company;
- **Ordinary Shareholder** means a holder of Ordinary Shares;
- **Ordinary Share** means an ordinary share in the capital of the Company with the rights set out in clause 2.2;
- **Preference Share** means a preference share in the capital of the Company with the rights set out in clause 2.3 or otherwise determined by the Board on issue;

## 2. Share capital

### 2.1 Classes of shares
The share capital of the Company is divided into the following classes:
- Ordinary Shares;
- such other classes of Preference Shares (including seed preferred, Series A preferred, and any Founder Shares) as the Board may determine from time to time under clause 2.4;
- **Initial share classes on adoption:** {{share_classes}}.

Each Share, once fully paid, ranks equally with every other Share of the same class in respect of all rights and obligations, except as expressly provided in this Constitution or in the terms of issue of that class.

### 2.2 Rights attaching to Ordinary Shares
Each Ordinary Share confers on its holder:
1. the right to receive notice of, attend, and vote at general meetings — one vote per Share on a poll and one vote per member on a show of hands (per s250E);
2. the right to receive dividends declared by the Board pro rata to the number of Ordinary Shares held;
3. the right, on a winding up, to participate in the surplus assets of the Company pro rata to the number of Ordinary Shares held, after payment of all debts and any liquidation preference payable on Preference Shares;
4. the pre-emptive right on new issues set out in clause 3;
5. the transfer restrictions and drag/tag rights set out in clauses 4, 5 and 6.

### 2.3 Rights attaching to Preference Shares
The Board may issue Preference Shares (including without limitation seed preferred and Series A preferred shares) on any terms it determines under s254A of the Corporations Act, provided the terms of issue set out clearly:
1. the entitlement to dividends (cumulative or non-cumulative, participating or non-participating);
2. the liquidation preference (whether 1x non-participating, 1x participating, or otherwise) and its priority in a winding up or Liquidity Event;
3. the entitlement to vote;
4. any conversion or anti-dilution rights;
5. the redemption rights (if any); and
6. any protective (reserved-matter) rights, which take effect subject to clause 8.14.

### 2.4 Board power to issue Shares
Subject to the Corporations Act, this Constitution, and any Shareholders' Agreement executed by the Company, the Board may issue Shares on such terms as it determines, provided the pre-emptive rights in clause 3 have been observed.

### 2.5 Buy-backs
The Company may buy back its own Shares in accordance with Division 2 of Part 2J.1 of the Corporations Act. All such buy-backs require prior Board approval by resolution, and where required by law a Shareholder resolution.

### 2.6 Financial assistance
The Company must not give financial assistance to acquire Shares in the Company except in accordance with s260A of the Corporations Act.

### 2.7 Variation of class rights
The rights attached to any class of Shares may be varied only by (a) a Special Resolution of the Company and (b) a Special Resolution of the holders of the class affected, in accordance with s246B of the Corporations Act.

## 3. Pre-emptive rights on new issues

### 3.1 Offer to existing Shareholders
Before issuing any new Shares (or any securities convertible into Shares), the Company must first offer them to each Ordinary Shareholder in proportion to that Shareholder's holding of Ordinary Shares (on a fully diluted basis). The offer must:
1. be in writing;
2. specify the number of Shares offered, the price, and the material terms;
3. remain open for acceptance for at least **21 days**.

### 3.2 Exclusions
Clause 3.1 does not apply to Shares (or options) issued:
1. under an employee share option or share plan approved by the Board (up to the aggregate reserve pool authorised by Shareholders);
2. as consideration in a bona fide acquisition of a business or company approved by the Board;
3. on the conversion of any convertible security (SAFE, convertible note, or preference share) issued in compliance with clause 3.1 at the time of its original issue;
4. under a bona fide equity financing where the Board determines that speed of closing requires waiver, provided that the waiver is subsequently approved by Special Resolution of the members within 6 months.

### 3.3 Unaccepted shares
Shares not accepted by an existing Shareholder within the offer period may, within 90 days after the close of the offer, be issued by the Board to any person on terms no more favourable than those offered to Shareholders.

## 4. Transfer of Shares

### 4.1 Board approval
The Board may, in its absolute discretion and without giving reasons, refuse to register a transfer of Shares. In exercising this discretion, the Board must act in the best interests of the Company as a whole.

### 4.2 Instrument of transfer
An instrument of transfer must be in the standard form under s1071B of the Corporations Act (or such other form as the Board approves), executed by both transferor and transferee, and delivered to the Company together with the share certificate (if any).

### 4.3 Pre-emptive right on transfer (right of first refusal)
Before transferring any Shares to a third party (other than a Permitted Transferee under clause 4.5), a Shareholder (the **Selling Shareholder**) must first offer them to the other Ordinary Shareholders as follows:
1. The Selling Shareholder gives written notice to the Company specifying the number of Shares offered, the price (in cash), and the identity of the proposed third-party transferee;
2. The Company forwards the offer within 5 Business Days to all other Ordinary Shareholders, who then have **30 days** to accept in whole or in part in proportion to their existing holding;
3. If the offer is not fully accepted, the Selling Shareholder may transfer the unaccepted Shares to the identified third party within 90 days on terms no more favourable than those offered.

### 4.4 Deemed transfer events
A Shareholder is deemed to have offered all their Shares under clause 4.3 (at the fair market value determined by an independent expert appointed by the Board) upon:
1. bankruptcy, insolvency, or the appointment of a trustee in bankruptcy;
2. in the case of an employee-Shareholder, cessation of employment as a **Bad Leaver** (as defined in any applicable employee share plan or Shareholders' Agreement);
3. any attempted transfer in breach of this Constitution.

### 4.5 Permitted Transferees
Clauses 4.1 and 4.3 do not apply to a transfer by a Shareholder to:
1. a family trust of which the Shareholder is the sole trustee and the primary beneficiary;
2. a wholly-owned holding entity of the Shareholder; or
3. a spouse or child of the Shareholder,
provided the transferee first executes a deed of accession agreeing to be bound by this Constitution and any Shareholders' Agreement.

## 5. Drag-along

### 5.1 Drag-along right
If Ordinary Shareholders holding at least **{{drag_along_threshold}}%** (default 75%) of the issued Ordinary Shares (the **Dragging Shareholders**) wish to accept a bona fide arm's-length offer from a third party for 100% of the Shares in the Company (a **Drag Sale**), they may require all other Shareholders (the **Dragged Shareholders**) to sell all of their Shares on the same terms and conditions.

### 5.2 Notice
The Dragging Shareholders give written notice to the Company and each Dragged Shareholder specifying:
1. the identity of the third-party acquirer;
2. the price per Share and total consideration;
3. all other material terms; and
4. the completion date (which must be at least 20 Business Days after the notice).

### 5.3 Same-form consideration
The Dragged Shareholders receive the same form and amount of consideration per Share as the Dragging Shareholders (subject to any liquidation preference or Waterfall applicable to Preference Shares under clause 2.3).

### 5.4 Power of attorney
Each Shareholder irrevocably appoints the Company (acting by any director) as their attorney to execute all transfer documents required to give effect to a Drag Sale in accordance with this clause.

## 6. Tag-along

### 6.1 Tag-along right
If Ordinary Shareholders holding more than 50% of the issued Ordinary Shares (the **Selling Group**) propose to sell all or part of their Shares to a bona fide third party in a single transaction or series of related transactions, every other Ordinary Shareholder (each a **Tagging Shareholder**) has the right (but not the obligation) to require the Selling Group to procure that the third-party acquirer also purchases from the Tagging Shareholder the same proportion of their Shares on the same terms and conditions.

### 6.2 Notice and election
The Selling Group must give at least 20 Business Days' written notice of the proposed sale. A Tagging Shareholder exercises the tag-along right by written notice to the Selling Group within 10 Business Days.

### 6.3 Interaction with drag-along
Clause 6 does not apply where the drag-along right in clause 5 has been validly exercised.

## 7. Dividends

### 7.1 Declaration
The Board may from time to time declare and pay dividends out of profits legally available for distribution, provided the Company would satisfy the solvency test in s254T of the Corporations Act immediately after the payment.

### 7.2 Interim dividends
The Board may pay interim dividends without a general meeting.

### 7.3 Payment
Dividends are payable pro rata by reference to the amount paid up on each Share held (per s254W), subject to the priorities of any Preference Shares and to any franking policy adopted by the Board.

### 7.4 Dividends in specie
The Board may resolve to satisfy a dividend, in whole or in part, by the distribution of specific assets (including shares in another body corporate, bonds, or property), and may fix the value of those assets for that purpose in a manner it considers fair.

### 7.5 Unclaimed dividends
Dividends unclaimed for 3 years may be forfeited by resolution of the Board and returned to the profits of the Company, subject to any applicable unclaimed money legislation.

### 7.6 Dividend reinvestment
The Board may, but is not required to, establish a dividend reinvestment plan on terms it considers appropriate.

## 8. Directors

### 8.1 Number
The Company must have at least one director who ordinarily resides in Australia (per s201A). The Board may from time to time determine the maximum number of directors, subject to a minimum of 1 and a maximum of 10 unless otherwise agreed by Special Resolution.

### 8.2 Appointment
Directors may be appointed:
1. by ordinary resolution of the Shareholders;
2. by the existing Board to fill a casual vacancy or to add a director up to the maximum permitted (subject to confirmation at the next Annual General Meeting or Written Resolution); or
3. as expressly nominated under any Shareholders' Agreement (each such director being an **Investor Director** or **Founder Director** as applicable).

### 8.3 First directors
The first directors of the Company on adoption of this Constitution are: {{first_directors}}.

### 8.4 Removal
A director may be removed:
1. by ordinary resolution of the Shareholders under s203C (for a proprietary company);
2. automatically on the events set out in s206B of the Corporations Act (bankruptcy, disqualification, mental incapacity, death); or
3. where the director was nominated by a Shareholder or class of Shareholders under a Shareholders' Agreement, by the nominating party removing that appointment in writing.

### 8.5 Powers of the Board
The business of the Company is managed by or under the direction of the Board (per s198A). The Board may exercise all powers of the Company except those required by the Corporations Act or this Constitution to be exercised by the Company in general meeting.

### 8.6 Chair
The Board must elect a Chair from among its number. The Chair presides at all Board meetings and general meetings at which they are present.

{{#casting_vote_yes}}
### 8.7 Casting vote (variant — casting vote enabled)
In the event of an equality of votes at a meeting of the Board, the Chair has a second or **casting vote**, in addition to their deliberative vote. The Chair also has a casting vote in the event of an equality of votes at a general meeting of the Company.
{{/casting_vote_yes}}

{{#casting_vote_no}}
### 8.7 Casting vote (variant — casting vote disabled)
The Chair does **not** have a casting vote at meetings of the Board or at general meetings. In the event of an equality of votes, the resolution is lost, and the matter must be re-tabled or resolved through the deadlock resolution mechanism in clause 8.15.
{{/casting_vote_no}}

### 8.8 Board meetings
The Board may meet, adjourn, and regulate its meetings as it thinks fit. Reasonable notice of each meeting (not less than 5 Business Days unless waived by all directors) must be given, together with an agenda.

### 8.9 Quorum
The quorum for a Board meeting is a majority of the directors then in office and must include at least one Investor Director (if any Investor Director has been appointed and is not disqualified under a conflict-of-interest rule).

### 8.10 Attendance by technology
A Board meeting may be held with directors participating by any technology (video, audio, or otherwise) provided each director can hear every other participating director.

### 8.11 Written resolutions of directors
The Board may pass a written resolution signed (in counterparts, and including by electronic signature) by all directors entitled to vote on the matter. The resolution takes effect on the date the last signature is affixed.

### 8.12 Board committees
The Board may delegate any of its powers to a committee of directors (audit, remuneration, or otherwise). A committee must exercise its delegated powers in accordance with any directions given by the Board.

### 8.13 Interested directors
A director who has a material personal interest in a matter being considered by the Board must give notice of that interest under s191 of the Corporations Act and must not vote on the matter or be counted in the quorum for it, except where s194 permits.

### 8.14 Reserved matters
The following matters (**Reserved Matters**) require, in addition to Board approval, the approval of Shareholders holding at least {{reserved_matter_threshold}}% (default 75%) of the Ordinary Shares:
1. amending or repealing this Constitution;
2. creating any new class of Shares or altering the rights attached to any class;
3. issuing Shares outside the ESOP or a bona fide equity financing on standard AU market terms;
4. selling, leasing or otherwise disposing of the whole or substantially the whole of the business or assets of the Company;
5. approving any Change of Control transaction;
6. voluntarily winding up the Company;
7. changing the auditor;
8. approving any related-party transaction outside the ordinary course of business;
9. incurring debt above A${{debt_ceiling}} in aggregate.

### 8.15 Deadlock resolution
If the Board is unable to pass a resolution on the same matter at two consecutive Board meetings due to an equality of votes (or, where clause 8.7 disables the casting vote, an inability to reach the required majority), the matter is a **Deadlock**. The Company must first refer the Deadlock to mediation under clause 15.1. If mediation fails within 30 days, any director may require the Board to convene a general meeting to seek a Shareholder resolution on the matter.

### 8.16 Remuneration
Directors may be paid remuneration for their services as determined by the Shareholders in general meeting. Non-executive directors are entitled to reimbursement of reasonable expenses. Executive directors may be paid such salary and benefits as the Board determines.

### 8.17 Alternate directors
A director may, with the approval of the Board, appoint an alternate to act in their place. The alternate has the same rights (including voting) as the appointing director when acting in that capacity.

## 9. General meetings

### 9.1 Convening
The Board may convene a general meeting whenever it thinks fit. The Board must convene a meeting on the requisition of Shareholders holding at least 5% of the issued votes (per s249D).

### 9.2 Notice
At least **21 days'** notice in writing (or such shorter period as permitted under s249H and consented to by the requisite majority of Shareholders) must be given of each general meeting. The notice must include the date, time, place, business to be conducted, and (for a special resolution) the intention to propose it as a special resolution.

### 9.3 Quorum
The quorum for a general meeting is 2 Shareholders (or their proxies or corporate representatives) present in person or by technology, together holding at least 50% of the issued Voting Shares. If a quorum is not present within 30 minutes after the scheduled start time, the meeting is adjourned to the same day, time, and place in the following week, at which the Shareholders present constitute a quorum.

### 9.4 Voting
Voting is by show of hands unless a poll is demanded by (a) the Chair, (b) at least 5 members present in person or by proxy, or (c) any member holding at least 5% of the issued votes (per s250L). On a poll, each Voting Share carries one vote.

### 9.5 Proxies
A Shareholder entitled to attend and vote at a general meeting may appoint a proxy (per s249X). A proxy need not be a Shareholder. A proxy form must be received by the Company at least 48 hours before the meeting to be valid, and may be signed electronically.

### 9.6 Corporate representatives
A Shareholder that is a body corporate may appoint a natural person to act as its corporate representative under s250D.

### 9.7 Meetings by technology
A general meeting may be held wholly or partly by technology in accordance with ss249R–249T. The reasonable opportunity to participate must be provided to all Shareholders entitled to attend.

### 9.8 Chair of meeting
The Chair of the Board presides at each general meeting. If the Chair is absent or unwilling to act, the directors present may elect one of their number to preside.

## 10. Written resolutions of Shareholders

### 10.1 Circulating resolution
A resolution of Shareholders (other than one required by the Corporations Act to be moved at a general meeting) may be passed by a circulating resolution in writing signed by all Shareholders entitled to vote on the resolution (per s249A). The resolution may be signed in counterparts, including by electronic signature.

### 10.2 Effect
A circulating resolution takes effect on the date the last required signature is affixed and is as valid and effective as a resolution passed at a duly convened general meeting.

## 11. Notices

### 11.1 Method of giving notice
Any notice under this Constitution may be given personally, by post to the last address recorded in the Register, or by electronic communication (email) to the last email address notified to the Company.

### 11.2 When notice is deemed given
A notice is deemed given:
1. if delivered personally, when delivered;
2. if posted, on the third Business Day after posting;
3. if sent by email, at the time recorded on the sender's system as the time the email left the sender's information system, provided no bounce-back is received within 4 hours.

## 12. Indemnity and insurance

### 12.1 Indemnity
To the extent permitted by law (and subject to s199A and s199B of the Corporations Act), the Company indemnifies each current and former director, secretary, and executive officer of the Company against:
1. any liability to another person (other than the Company or a related body corporate) incurred while acting in that capacity, unless the liability arises out of conduct involving a lack of good faith; and
2. legal costs incurred in defending or resisting proceedings in which the person becomes involved because of that capacity, provided the person is not found guilty and no order of a court against them is made in the proceedings.

### 12.2 Insurance
To the extent permitted by law (and subject to s199B of the Corporations Act), the Company may pay premiums for insurance for each current and former director, secretary, and executive officer against liability incurred by them in that capacity, including legal costs.

## 13. Winding up

### 13.1 Distribution of surplus
On a winding up of the Company, the liquidator must apply the surplus assets in the following order:
1. first, in payment of any accrued but unpaid dividends and any liquidation preference on Preference Shares in accordance with their terms of issue;
2. second, in return of the amount paid up on Preference Shares (unless already returned under paragraph 1);
3. third, in return of the amount paid up on Ordinary Shares;
4. finally, in payment of the balance to Ordinary Shareholders pro rata to their holdings of Ordinary Shares.

### 13.2 Distribution in specie
With the sanction of a Special Resolution, the liquidator may distribute among the Shareholders in kind the whole or any part of the property of the Company, and may set the value of the property for that purpose.

## 14. Secretary and seal

### 14.1 Secretary
The Company may (but need not — see s204A for proprietary companies) appoint one or more secretaries. Each secretary must ordinarily reside in Australia.

### 14.2 Seal
The Company need not have a common seal. If it adopts one, the seal must be used only by the authority of the Board. The seal must be affixed to a document in the presence of two directors, or one director and one secretary, each of whom must sign the document (per s127(2)).

### 14.3 Execution without seal
Documents may be executed by the Company without a seal by the signatures required under s127(1) of the Corporations Act.

## 15. Dispute resolution

### 15.1 Mediation first
Any dispute arising out of or in connection with this Constitution (including any Deadlock under clause 8.15) must first be referred to mediation administered by the Australian Disputes Centre (or such other mediation service as the parties agree) in accordance with its Guidelines for Commercial Mediation.

### 15.2 Arbitration
If the dispute is not resolved by mediation within 30 days after the appointment of the mediator, either party may refer the dispute to arbitration administered by the Australian Centre for International Commercial Arbitration (ACICA) under the ACICA Arbitration Rules. The seat of the arbitration is Sydney, New South Wales. The language is English. The number of arbitrators is one, unless the amount in dispute exceeds A$500,000, in which case there must be three.

### 15.3 Urgent relief
Nothing in this clause 15 prevents a party from seeking urgent injunctive or declaratory relief from a court of competent jurisdiction in New South Wales.

### 15.4 Continuing performance
Notwithstanding a dispute, each party must continue to perform its obligations under this Constitution during the dispute resolution process.
