## 5. Limb 2c — ATO private binding ruling fallback

Instead of Limb 2a (100-point) or Limb 2b (principles-based), the
company may obtain a **private binding ruling** from the ATO under
*Taxation Administration Act 1953* (Cth) Sch 1 Div 359 confirming that
it meets the innovation limb. This is the slowest route (typically
28-90 days) but provides the highest certainty for downstream investor
reliance.

**Founder note:** an ATO Product Ruling (e.g. **TR 2019/1**) sets the
framework but is **not** a substitute for a company-specific PBR — a
PBR binds the Commissioner to a specific taxpayer on a specific set of
facts and is the gold-standard evidence for an investor's tax adviser.

## 7. Investor communication guardrails

Before circulating this result to any prospective investor:

1. **Never state "ESIC-qualifying" without a signed letter from the
   TPB-registered tax agent named in Section 1.** BlockID's worksheet
   is a *self-assessment aid* — an investor relying on ESIC status for
   the 20% Div 360 offset or 10-year CGT exemption will require their
   own tax adviser to obtain a sign-off letter or view the ATO PBR.
2. **6-month staleness convention.** ESIC status is assessed against a
   specific income year; a worksheet older than 6 months should be
   treated as stale and re-run before circulating to a new investor.
3. **Corps Act 2001 (Cth) s1041H + s769C exposure.** Misleading
   statements about the tax character of a financial product are
   actionable regardless of intent — do not paraphrase or round up the
   Limb 2a score, and never omit a failed Limb 1 gateway from investor
   materials.
4. **Australian Consumer Law s18 exposure.** Even for non-financial
   promotional materials (e.g. a "Come invest with us — ESIC benefits!"
   landing page), misrepresenting ESIC status is misleading and
   deceptive conduct in trade.
5. **This worksheet is NOT a tax ruling.** Investors claiming the Div
   360 offset or 10-year CGT exemption on qualifying shares held ≥ 12
   months must satisfy themselves (through their own tax adviser) that
   the company met the tests **at the time the shares were issued** —
   the investor's tax position is not the founder's decision and cannot
   be warranted by the founder without exposure under s1041H.
