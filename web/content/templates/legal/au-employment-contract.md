## 5. Leave (National Employment Standards)

The Employee's leave entitlements are those set out in the NES:

- **Annual leave** — 4 weeks per year of service (5 weeks for a shift worker as defined) accruing progressively (s87);
- **Personal / carer's leave** — 10 days per year of service accruing progressively (s96);
- **Compassionate leave** — 2 days per permissible occasion (s104);
- **Family and domestic violence leave** — 10 days per year, available in full from day one, non-accruing (ss106A–106E);
- **Community service leave** — as required under s108 (jury service is paid for up to 10 days per s111);
- **Long service leave** — as provided by the applicable State / Territory legislation (in NSW, the *Long Service Leave Act 1955* — 2 months paid leave after 10 years of continuous service);
- **Parental leave** — up to 12 months unpaid, with the right to request a further 12 months (ss70–85), separate from any government Paid Parental Leave scheme entitlement;
- **Public holidays** — paid absence per s114 in respect of the public holidays listed in s115.

Leave requests must be made in writing (email is sufficient) and are approved subject to operational requirements.

## 6. Employee obligations

### 6.1 Best efforts
The Employee will devote their working hours to the Employer's business, act in the best interests of the Employer, and follow all reasonable and lawful directions.

### 6.2 Policies
The Employee will comply with the Employer's workplace policies as amended from time to time, including the code of conduct, WHS policy, anti-discrimination and sexual-harassment policy, and IT-acceptable-use policy. Policies do not form part of this Agreement and do not create contractual entitlements.

### 6.3 Fitness for work
The Employee will attend work fit for duty and free from the effects of alcohol or unlawful drugs.

### 6.4 Conflicts of interest
The Employee will disclose in writing any actual, potential, or perceived conflict of interest, including secondary employment, board positions, or financial interests in a competitor, customer, or supplier.

## 7. Confidentiality

### 7.1 Confidential Information
"**Confidential Information**" means all non-public information of the Employer or any related body corporate (as defined in s50 of the *Corporations Act 2001* (Cth)), including business plans, customer lists, pricing, source code, algorithms, trade secrets, technical know-how, and personnel information.

### 7.2 Obligation
The Employee must not, during or after employment, disclose, use, or copy Confidential Information except (a) as required to perform the Employee's duties, (b) with the Employer's prior written consent, or (c) as required by law (in which case the Employee will notify the Employer as soon as practicable).

### 7.3 Return of property
On termination, the Employee will return all Employer property (including Confidential Information in any form) and delete any copy in the Employee's personal possession.

## 8. Intellectual property

### 8.1 Assignment
The Employee assigns to the Employer, with effect from creation, all right, title, and interest (including all future copyright and other intellectual-property rights) in all works, inventions, designs, and creations made by the Employee in the course of employment or using the Employer's resources (the **Work Product**).

### 8.2 Moral rights
The Employee consents (to the maximum extent permitted by Part IX of the *Copyright Act 1968* (Cth)) to any act or omission of the Employer that would otherwise infringe the Employee's moral rights in the Work Product.

### 8.3 Further assurances
The Employee will do any act and sign any document reasonably required by the Employer to give effect to this section 8, at the Employer's cost.

### 8.4 Pre-existing IP

### 9.1 Non-compete
For **{{restraint_period}}** after the end of employment, and within the geographic area of **{{restraint_geography}}**, the Employee will not, in the same industry as the Employer, be employed by or engaged as a director, consultant, or contractor of any business that competes with the Employer for **{{restraint_scope}}**.

## 12. Termination

### 12.1 Notice
Either party may end the employment by giving notice in writing:
- during probation — 1 week;
- after probation — **{{post_probation_notice_weeks}}** weeks, or the notice period required by s117 of the *Fair Work Act 2009* for the Employee's length of service, whichever is greater. The Employee is entitled to an additional 1 week of notice from the Employer if aged over 45 with at least 2 years of continuous service (s117(3)(b)).

### 12.2 Payment in lieu
The Employer may pay the Employee in lieu of all or part of the notice period (base salary only, excluding superannuation, unless required by the applicable Award).

### 12.3 Summary termination
The Employer may terminate immediately without notice for serious misconduct as defined in reg 1.07 of the *Fair Work Regulations 2009*.

### 12.4 Redundancy
Where the Employee's position becomes genuinely redundant, redundancy pay is calculated per s119 of the *Fair Work Act 2009* by reference to length of continuous service (subject to the small-business-employer exemption in s121 for employers with fewer than 15 employees).

### 12.5 Final payments
On termination the Employer will pay the Employee: (a) all outstanding wages and superannuation; (b) accrued but untaken annual leave (s90); (c) accrued but untaken long service leave where a State entitlement has arisen; and (d) any Award or NES entitlement to notice, payment in lieu, or redundancy.
